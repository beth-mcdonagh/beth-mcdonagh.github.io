## Tides and internal waves
My PhD thesis was related to barotropic and internal tides in the Mediterranean Sea and their interactions with other phenomena across spatial and temporal scales. I believe that there are several outstanding questions in this area that deserve further investigation, such as the following:
- How are internal tides dissipated in the Mediterranean Sea?
- We know that seiches are important in the Mediterranean Sea, that they interact with tides, and that it's likely that they have baroclinic components. How do these interact with internal tides? How is this energy distributed in the ocean?
- The Mediterranean Sea has many long-lasting eddies and gyres, including in some key regions for internal tide generation/propagation. How do they interact and dissipate internal tides?
- How do (internal) tides affect the Mediterranean Sea’s overturning circulation? 


## Ocean variability and predictability
One of my projects as a postdoc at CMCC involves developing a new ensemble forecasting system for the Mediterranean Sea, in which I am investigating methods of perturbation while considering how this fits into the broader theories of ocean variability and predictability. Some of the research questions that I am working towards answering are these:
- Which parts of the model and its external forcings can be perturbed to improve forecast skill?
- Operational ensemble forecasting in the short-medium range in the ocean is much less common than in the atmosphere. The relatively long memory of the ocean compared to the atmosphere contributes to slower divergence of ensemble members for a given perturbation compared to in the atmosphere. How can we generate an ensemble whose members cover the range of uncertainty despite this?
- What other unique challenges are there for ensemble forecasting in the ocean, and how can we address them?
- What can an ensemble such as this tell us about ocean variability in the Mediterranean Sea?


## Ocean modelling
My other current project is the development of a nested modelling approach to reach kilometre-scale horizontal resolution in the Gibraltar Strait within an operational forecasting model of the Mediterranean Sea, specifically using NEMO + AGRIF to achieve this. The Gibraltar Strait is a highly important region as the only connection from the Mediterranean Sea to the rest of the global ocean, and is challenging to model accurately due to its narrow channel, steep ridges, complex vertical mixing, hydraulic control points, and lack of consistent observational data. 
- What are the advantages and limitations of NEMO + AGRIF for high-resolution modelling challenges? 
- Can the additional computational costs of a model such as this be justified by improved model skill due to the higher resolution?
- What can a kilometre scale nested model of this region tell us about the Gibraltar Strait and its influence on the Mediterranean Sea?
- Are there advantages to further increasing horizontal and vertical resolution without moving towards a non-hydrostatic model?
